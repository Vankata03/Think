# Foundation Models on current iOS (wayfinder #59)

Research date: 2026-09-15. Part of #58. Sources are Apple primary documentation only: developer.apple.com reference and articles, WWDC session pages, Apple Machine Learning Research, Apple Support and Apple Newsroom. Anything not confirmed by one of those is marked **Unverified**.

## Short answer

iOS 27 shipped on 2026-09-14, so the App Store now targets iOS 27 and the Foundation Models framework has two model tiers: the on-device `SystemLanguageModel` (available since iOS 26.0, replaced by a new model version in 27.0) and the server-side `PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel` (new in 27.0, needs a managed entitlement). Think's deployment target of iOS 26.0 covers the on-device model on every Apple Intelligence device; 26.0–26.3, 26.4 and 27.0 run three different model versions, so prompts need testing against each. Bulgarian is not supported; German, Spanish, French, Italian and Portuguese are. The on-device context window is 4,096 tokens shared between input and output, and overflow throws `LanguageModelError.contextSizeExceeded`. The framework exists on watchOS only from watchOS 27 and only through Private Cloud Compute or a custom model; the on-device `SystemLanguageModel` is not available on watchOS.

## 1. Has iOS 27 shipped, and what changed versus iOS 26

- iOS 27 was released on 2026-09-14. Apple's support page "Apple Intelligence: Key Requirements & Availability" was updated 2026-09-14 and lists iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, visionOS 27 and watchOS 27 as the required OS versions. <https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115>
- Apple Newsroom (2026-06) announced that the new software features ship "as a free software update this fall". <https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/apple-unveils-next-generation-of-apple-intelligence-siri-ai-and-more/>
- The Foundation Models "updates" page (June 2026 section) lists the iOS 27 changes: a new on-device `SystemLanguageModel` "that follows instructions more accurately and produces better results", the `LanguageModel` protocol for any server or on-device model, `PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel` "to access more reasoning capabilities and a larger context size", image attachments in prompts, `DynamicProfile`, `ToolCallingMode`, an updated Instruments template, and new error types (`LanguageModelError`, `SystemLanguageModel.Error`, `LanguageModelSession.Error`). <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/Updates/FoundationModels>
- The same page's February 2026 section records the iOS 26.4 changes: a second on-device model version, "improved guardrails" that "reduce the possibility of blocking benign content", `tokenCount(for:)`, and `contextSize`. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/Updates/FoundationModels>
- `SystemLanguageModel` reference: "Currently, there are 3 model versions that align with: iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and visionOS 26.0 - 26.3; iOS, iPadOS, macOS, visionOS 26.4; iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and visionOS 27.0." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel>
- Apple Machine Learning Research (2026-06-08) describes the third-generation models: AFM 3 Core, "the next generation of our 3-billion-parameter dense model", and AFM 3 Core Advanced, a "20-billion-parameter model [that] uses a sparse architecture, activating just 1 to 4 billion parameters at a time" and is "unlocked by and optimized for our most capable Apple silicon systems". <https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-third-generation-of-apple-foundation-models>
- The iOS 26 on-device model was "a large language model with 3 billion parameters, each quantized to 2 bits" (WWDC25 session 286). <https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/>
- `LanguageModelSession.GenerationError` (iOS 26.0) is deprecated in 27.0 in favour of `LanguageModelError`. Its cases were `assetsUnavailable`, `decodingFailure`, `exceededContextWindowSize`, `guardrailViolation`, `rateLimited`, `refusal`, `concurrentRequests`, `unsupportedGuide`, `unsupportedLanguageOrLocale`. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelsession/generationerror>
- Which AFM 3 model (Core vs Core Advanced) a given iPhone gets through `SystemLanguageModel` is **Unverified**; Apple's developer documentation does not name the model behind the API. The WWDC26 "What's new" session says the iOS 26.4 context-size APIs let you "adapt your app to the hardware it's running on" and its code sample prints `model.contextSize // 8192`, which implies the context size can differ by device on iOS 27. <https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/>

## 2. Devices and OS versions that can run the on-device model

- Framework availability: iOS 26.0+, iPadOS 26.0+, Mac Catalyst 26.0+, macOS 26.0+, visionOS 26.0+, watchOS 27.0+. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels>
- "To use Apple Foundation Models, people need a device that supports Apple Intelligence." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels>
- Supported devices (Apple Support, updated 2026-09-14): "iPhone 16 models or later, iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, iPhone Air, iPad mini (A17 Pro), MacBook Neo (A18 Pro), iPad models with M1 or later, Mac with M1 or later, Apple Vision Pro, Apple Watch Series 9 or later, Apple Watch Ultra 2 or later, and Apple Watch SE 3". Apple Watch models qualify "when paired with an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone; some features require iPhone nearby." <https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115>
- Storage: up to 14 GB on iPhone 17 Pro/Pro Max and newer iPad/Mac models, up to 8 GB on other compatible devices, up to 1.5 GB on Apple Watch. <https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115>
- Regions: "Apple Intelligence features will not currently work for supported devices purchased in China mainland." EU residents get the features with iOS/iPadOS 27, macOS 27 and visionOS 27. <https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115>
- Apple Intelligence must be enabled and the model downloaded; models "are downloaded automatically based on factors like network status, battery level, and system load." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel/availability-swift.enum/unavailablereason/modelnotready>
- Practical consequence for Think: the iPhone 15 (non-Pro) and every older iPhone that can install iOS 26 or 27 will report `.unavailable(.deviceNotEligible)`. Any AI feature needs a non-AI fallback.

## 3. Supported languages

- The on-device model "is multilingual, which means the same model understands and generates text in any language that Apple Intelligence supports." The article points to the "Supported languages" section of the Apple Support page. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/supporting-languages-and-locales-with-foundation-models>
- Apple Support (2026-09-14): "Apple Intelligence is available with support for these languages: English, Danish, Dutch, French, German, Italian, Norwegian, Portuguese, Spanish, Swedish, Turkish, Vietnamese, Chinese (simplified), Chinese (traditional), Japanese, and Korean." visionOS 27 excludes Danish, Dutch, Norwegian, Swedish, Turkish and Vietnamese. <https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115>
- Apple Newsroom (2025-03) named the Portuguese variant when it launched: "French, German, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil), Spanish, Japanese, Korean, and Chinese (simplified)" with iOS 18.4. <https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/03/apple-intelligence-features-expand-to-new-languages-and-regions-today/>
- Verdict for Think's seven locales: en, de, es, fr, it and pt-BR are covered. **Bulgarian is not supported.** It does not appear on the Apple Support list, the Newsroom list, or in the locale groups the AFM 3 report evaluates (PFIGSCJK, DDNSTV, and AFIHHMPRTU = Arabic, Finnish, Indonesian, Hebrew, Hindi, Malay, Polish, Russian, Thai, Ukrainian). <https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-third-generation-of-apple-foundation-models>
- The AFM 3 report evaluates ten locales (AFIHHMPRTU) that the Apple Support list does not yet include; whether those are exposed to apps on iOS 27 is **Unverified**. The Support page is the authoritative shipped list.
- Runtime checks: `SystemLanguageModel.default.supportsLocale(_:)` (defaults to `Locale.current`, honours per-app language settings, and accepts close variants such as en-AU) and `supportedLanguages` for the full list. If the prompt contains an unsupported language the session throws `unsupportedLanguageOrLocale`. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/supporting-languages-and-locales-with-foundation-models>
- Guardrails are language-bound: "Guardrails for model input and output safety are only for supported languages and locales. If a prompt contains sensitive content in an unsupported language, which typically is a short phrase mixed-in with text in a supported language, it might not throw a `unsupportedLanguageOrLocale` error. If unsupported-language detection fails, the guardrails may also fail to flag that short, unsupported content." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/supporting-languages-and-locales-with-foundation-models>
- Locale steering: for non-US-English locales Apple recommends the exact instruction phrase "The person's locale is \(locale.identifier)." and an explicit output-language instruction such as "You MUST respond in Italian", because "by default, the model responds in the language or languages of its inputs." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/supporting-languages-and-locales-with-foundation-models>
- Prompts may be kept in Xcode string catalogs and localized per language, which "can help the model respond more reliably in the correct language." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/updating-prompts-for-new-model-versions>

## 4. Context window and overflow

- "Apple's on-device foundation model has a context window of 4096 tokens per session, with a token representing each word, or partial word." The window covers "all prompts, instructions, tool definitions and their input and output, generable type schemas, and all of the model's responses." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/managing-the-context-window>
- `contextSize` "represents the total number of tokens that can be used in a single session, including both input prompts and generated responses." It throws if the model is unavailable or Apple Intelligence is disabled. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel/contextsize>
- Token density: "In Latin alphabet languages such as English, a token typically represents three to four characters." The overview article names Spanish and German alongside English for that ratio. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/managing-the-context-window>, <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generating-content-and-performing-tasks-with-foundation-models>
- Private Cloud Compute comparison table: on-device 4K context, no reasoning, unlimited use, works offline; PCC 32K context, multiple reasoning levels, daily limit, needs network. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/adding-server-side-intelligence-with-private-cloud-compute>
- Overflow: "After reaching the context size, the session can no longer process additional requests and throws an `contextSizeExceeded(_:)` error. When this happens, trim the session history or create a new session." Catch pattern: `catch LanguageModelError.contextSizeExceeded(let context)`. On iOS 26.0–26.x the equivalent case was `LanguageModelSession.GenerationError.exceededContextWindowSize`. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/managing-the-context-window>, <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelsession/generationerror>
- Measuring: `tokenCount(for:)` (iOS 26.4+) counts tokens for a prompt, instructions or transcript; the Foundation Models Instruments template shows consumed, generated and cached tokens per request. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/Updates/FoundationModels>, <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/analyzing-the-runtime-performance-of-your-foundation-models-app>
- Apple's recommended strategies for large inputs: concise prompts ("no more than three paragraphs"), `@Guide(.maximumCount(_:))` on arrays, `maximumResponseTokens` only as a last resort, simple `Generable` types, `includeSchemaInPrompt: false` on repeat requests, and splitting work across sessions (chunk, summarize each chunk, combine). <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/managing-the-context-window>
- Implication for Think: a month of journal entries will not fit in 4,096 tokens alongside instructions and a schema. Pattern detection over many entries has to be a map/reduce over sessions or pre-filtered to a handful of entries per call.

## 5. watchOS

- Framework platforms include watchOS 27.0+. `LanguageModelSession`, `Generable`, `GenerationID`, `ResponseStream`, `LanguageModelError` and `prewarm(promptPrefix:)` all list watchOS 27.0. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels>, <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelsession>
- `SystemLanguageModel` (the on-device model), its `Availability` enum, `Guardrails`, `contextSize` and `supportedLanguages` list only iOS, iPadOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS and visionOS; watchOS is absent. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel>
- `PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel` lists watchOS 27.0+, and Apple states "Private Cloud Compute makes it possible for us to bring the Foundation Models framework to watchOS. Starting in watchOS 27, you can wear your most powerful intelligence features right on your wrist." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/privatecloudcomputelanguagemodel>, <https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/>
- Conclusion: on watchOS the framework runs only against PCC (or a custom `LanguageModel` provider). No on-device Apple model for ThinkWatch, and PCC needs the entitlement described in section 10. There is no framework on watchOS 26.

## 6. Availability check and unavailability reasons

- Check `SystemLanguageModel.default.availability` (or `isAvailable`). Cases: `.available`, `.unavailable(UnavailableReason)`. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel/availability-swift.enum>
- `UnavailableReason` cases: `appleIntelligenceNotEnabled` ("Apple Intelligence is not enabled on the system"), `deviceNotEligible` ("The device does not support Apple Intelligence"), `modelNotReady` ("The models aren't available on the user's device", downloaded automatically depending on network, battery and load). <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel/availability-swift.enum/unavailablereason>
- Apple's sample switches over the three cases plus a default `.unavailable(let other)` branch, so code should not assume the enum is closed. "It can take some time for the model to download and become available when a person turns on Apple Intelligence." "Always verify model availability first, and plan for a fallback experience." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generating-content-and-performing-tasks-with-foundation-models>
- Availability also depends on region (Apple Intelligence support) and, for the current model version, on OS version. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel>
- iOS 27 adds `SystemLanguageModel.Error.assetsUnavailable` for the case where a request runs but model assets are missing. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel/error>
- PCC has its own `availability` with `.systemNotReady` in addition to `.deviceNotEligible`. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/adding-server-side-intelligence-with-private-cloud-compute>
- Xcode: Product > Scheme > Edit Scheme > Run > Options has a "Simulated Apple Foundation Models Availability" menu (documented for PCC quota states). <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/adding-server-side-intelligence-with-private-cloud-compute>

## 7. Guardrails, refusals and emotional or negative personal text

- Two safety layers: models "trained to handle sensitive topics with care" and "Guardrails that aim to block harmful or sensitive content, such as self-harm, violence, and adult materials." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/improving-the-safety-of-generative-model-output>
- Guardrails check both input and output. "If either fails the safety check, the model session throws a `guardrailViolation(_:)` error." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/improving-the-safety-of-generative-model-output>
- Refusals are separate from guardrails. "The on-device `SystemLanguageModel` isn't suitable for all topics, and it may refuse to discuss sensitive subjects." For string output the refusal arrives as ordinary text ("Sorry, I can't help with that…") that "you might not be able to programmatically determine". For guided generation "there's no placeholder for a refusal message. Instead, the model throws a `refusal(_:)` error", and `refusal.explanation` can be awaited to get a user-facing reason. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/improving-the-safety-of-generative-model-output>
- Full `LanguageModelError` case list on iOS 27: `contextSizeExceeded`, `rateLimited`, `refusal`, `timeout`, `guardrailViolation`, `unsupportedCapability`, `unsupportedTranscriptContent`, `unsupportedGenerationGuide`, `unsupportedLanguageOrLocale`. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelerror>
- Permissive mode: `SystemLanguageModel(guardrails: .permissiveContentTransformations)` skips the guardrail checks for string responses only ("it never throws a `guardrailViolation(_:)` error when generating string responses"); with guided generation "the framework runs the default guardrails against model input and output as usual." Even in permissive mode the model itself may still refuse. Apple's stated use cases are tagging chat messages containing profanity and explaining study notes on sensitive topics. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/improving-the-safety-of-generative-model-output>
- Guardrail false positives were reduced in iOS 26.4 with "even more improvements in iOS 27". <https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/>, <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/Updates/FoundationModels>
- Apple gives no specific threshold for emotional, grief, anxiety or negative-mood journal text. The documented risk is explicit: self-harm is one of the named guardrail categories, so a journal entry that mentions self-harm can trip `guardrailViolation` on input, and a pattern-analysis request over such entries can throw `refusal` when using guided generation. How often ordinary sadness or frustration triggers either is **Unverified** and needs a prompt test set on-device.
- Apple's own example prompts for journaling are neutral and framed: "Generate a wholesome and empathetic journal prompt that helps this person reflect on their day. They said: \(userInput)". The recommended pattern is instructions that grant the model a role and domain ("You are an AI assistant for a personal finance app who can assist with…"), which "help the model more accurately assess the safety of a request" and reduce over-blocking. Instructions must never contain user text (prompt injection). <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/improving-the-safety-of-generative-model-output>
- Apple asks developers to run a risk assessment, keep adversarial prompt tests (with the Evaluations framework), re-run them on every OS model update, offer a way to report harmful output, and file guardrail gaps via Feedback Assistant with `logFeedbackAttachment(sentiment:issues:desiredOutput:)`. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/improving-the-safety-of-generative-model-output>
- Health and mental-health content: the Foundation Models documentation has no mental-health-specific guidance beyond the self-harm guardrail category. App Review guideline 1.4.1 applies only to "Medical apps that could provide inaccurate data or information, or that could be used for diagnosing or treating patients" and asks such apps to "remind users to check with a doctor". Whether a journaling app with mood pattern summaries falls under 1.4.1 is **Unverified**; treating output as reflection prompts rather than diagnosis keeps it out of that scope. <https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/>
- The Human Interface Guidelines page "Generative AI" is referenced from the safety article for design guidance; its body could not be fetched (JavaScript-rendered), so its specific recommendations are **Unverified**. <https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai>

## 8. Guided generation, streaming and citing input entries

- `@Generable` on structs, actors and enums; `@Guide(description:)` on stored properties. "The framework uses constrained sampling when generating output", which "prevents the model from producing malformed output and provides you with results as a type you define." Supported primitives: `Bool`, `Int`, `Float`, `Double`, `Decimal`, `Array`, `String`; nested `Generable` types and enums with associated values are allowed. Properties are generated in declaration order. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generating-swift-data-structures-with-guided-generation>
- Guides: `pattern(_:)` (regex), `element(_:)`, `count(_:)`, `constant(_:)`, `anyOf(_:)`, `range(_:)`, `minimum`, `maximum`, `minimumCount`, `maximumCount`. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generationguide>
- `DynamicGenerationSchema` builds a schema at runtime, including `anyOf: [...]` string enumerations, and the result is decoded from `GeneratedContent` with `value(_:forProperty:)`. Apple's example restricts the model to a restaurant's actual menu items. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generating-swift-data-structures-with-guided-generation>
- Each `Generable` type's JSON schema is sent to the model and counts against the context window; keep types small, names short, descriptions rare, and pass `includeSchemaInPrompt: false` after the first request in a session. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generable>, <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/analyzing-the-runtime-performance-of-your-foundation-models-app>
- Streaming: `streamResponse(to:generating:)` returns a `ResponseStream`, "an async sequence of snapshots of partially generated content". The `@Generable` macro synthesizes a `PartiallyGenerated` type with optional properties. "Instead of raw deltas, we stream snapshots." `collect()` gathers the final value. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelsession/responsestream>, <https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/>
- `GenerationID`: "A unique identifier that is stable for the duration of a response, but not across responses." It is for SwiftUI list identity while streaming, not for referencing app data. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generationid>
- Citing input entries: Apple documents no first-class citation or reference feature. The workable mechanism from the documented API is to label each entry in the prompt with a short token (for example `E1`, `E2`) and constrain an output property to those labels with `@Guide(.anyOf([...]))` or a `DynamicGenerationSchema` `anyOf` built at runtime from the entries in the prompt; constrained sampling then guarantees every returned reference is one of the supplied labels, and the app maps labels back to entry identifiers. Whether the model picks the *correct* label is a quality question, not a schema guarantee, and is **Unverified** without testing. Passing raw UUIDs is possible but costs tokens (symbols tokenize badly: a phone number "might use over ten tokens"). <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generationguide>, <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/analyzing-the-runtime-performance-of-your-foundation-models-app>
- A session handles one request at a time; a second concurrent call is a runtime error. Check `isResponding`. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generating-content-and-performing-tasks-with-foundation-models>
- The on-device model is documented as unsuitable for basic math, code generation and logical reasoning; summarization, entity extraction, classification and tagging are its strengths. A `contentTagging` use case (`SystemLanguageModel(useCase: .contentTagging)`) is backed by a built-in adapter. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generating-content-and-performing-tasks-with-foundation-models>, <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel/usecase>

## 9. App Review and privacy manifest

- App Review Guideline 5.1.2(i): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." On-device `SystemLanguageModel` shares nothing with third parties; PCC is Apple-operated. The guidelines contain no clause specific to Foundation Models, generative AI or LLM features. <https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/>
- Guideline 1.4.1 (medical apps) is quoted in section 7.
- Privacy manifest: the "Describing use of required reason API" page does not list Foundation Models, `SystemLanguageModel` or Apple Intelligence among required-reason API categories, so using the framework adds no required-reason entry. Whether an app must declare a data-collection type for on-device inference is not addressed by Apple's documentation; since no data leaves the device, nothing is collected. **Unverified** beyond the absence of any mention. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api>
- No entitlement is needed for the on-device model. PCC needs `com.apple.developer.private-cloud-compute`, a managed entitlement. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.private-cloud-compute>
- Instruments traces store prompts and responses unencrypted; Apple warns to handle trace files "in a manner consistent with" the developer agreement, which matters when the prompts contain journal entries. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/analyzing-the-runtime-performance-of-your-foundation-models-app>

## 10. Private Cloud Compute (iOS 27 only)

- `LanguageModelSession(model: PrivateCloudComputeLanguageModel())`; 32K-token context, reasoning levels `light`, `moderate`, `deep` via `ContextOptions(reasoningLevel:)`; requires network; daily request quota per user, extendable through iCloud+; `quotaLimitReached` error and `quotaUsage` status API. Available "on iOS 27, macOS 27, watchOS 27, and visionOS 27 or later". <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/adding-server-side-intelligence-with-private-cloud-compute>
- Developer eligibility: enrolled in the App Store Small Business Program, fewer than 2 million first-time downloads across all apps, and the entitlement assigned via a capability request; "no cloud API cost". Exceeding the threshold gives six months to migrate. <https://developer.apple.com/private-cloud-compute/>
- Apple's guidance: "Start with the on-device model and evaluate it with the Evaluations framework. If you determine your feature needs more reasoning capability or context size, then use PCC." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/adding-server-side-intelligence-with-private-cloud-compute>

## 11. Performance, battery and prewarming

- `prewarm(promptPrefix:)`: "Loads the resources required for this session into memory ahead of a request." Use it "when you have a strong signal that the user will interact with session within a few seconds", for example when typing begins, and "only use prewarm when you have a window of at least 1 second before the call to a respond method." Passing a known prompt prefix lets the system process it eagerly. It is not guaranteed to load "particularly if your app is running in the background or the system is under load." <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelsession/prewarm(promptprefix:)>
- Latency drivers: "More output tokens generally require more processing time", summarization is cheaper than generation, and "High token counts affect both initial processing time and memory usage." Shorter prompts ("using three sentences") speed up generation. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/analyzing-the-runtime-performance-of-your-foundation-models-app>, <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generating-content-and-performing-tasks-with-foundation-models>
- Instruments Foundation Models template reports total duration, response duration, time to first token, model loading time, tool time and token counts (consumed, generated, cached). Profile only when the device is not under thermal pressure. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/analyzing-the-runtime-performance-of-your-foundation-models-app>
- Prefix (KV) caching: cached input tokens are reused across requests in a session; there is a dedicated article "Optimizing key-value caching in language model sessions" (iOS 27). <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/optimizing-key-value-caching-in-language-model-sessions>
- Battery: Apple documents no battery figures for the on-device model. The only battery statement is that model downloads depend on "battery level". Energy cost of inference is **Unverified**; measure with Instruments on-device. <https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel/availability-swift.enum/unavailablereason/modelnotready>
- Model download after enabling Apple Intelligence is up to 8 GB (14 GB on the newest devices), which is why `modelNotReady` needs a fallback UI. <https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115>

## 12. Adapters and custom training

- The Foundation Models Adapter Training Toolkit is a Python workflow requiring "Mac with Apple silicon and at least 32GB memory, or Linux GPU machines" and Python 3.11+. Deployment needs the managed "Foundation Models Framework Adapter Entitlement" (`com.apple.developer.foundation-model-adapter`). <https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter/>
- "Each adapter is compatible with a single specific system model version" and must be retrained for every OS model version; each adapter is about 160 MB and must be hosted and delivered through Background Assets, not bundled. Toolkit 26.0.0 "is not compatible with macOS, iOS, iPadOS, or visionOS 27 and later." <https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter/>
- Apple's advice: "Before considering adapters, try to get the most out of the system model using prompt engineering or tool calling." Adapters are for when "Prompt engineering isn't achieving the required accuracy or consistency for your task." <https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter/>
- Verdict for Think: not relevant. Three live model versions (26.0–26.3, 26.4, 27.0) would mean three adapters, each 160 MB, hosted server-side, retrained on every OS update, plus an entitlement request. Prompt engineering, `@Generable` schemas and the built-in `contentTagging` adapter cover a journaling app.
